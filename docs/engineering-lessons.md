# Engineering Lessons

## Learning Through Practice

When I started working on this system, I had very limited knowledge of the full industrial stack.

Most things seemed complex and unclear at first.

However, through hands-on work, debugging and experimentation, I gradually understood how the system works.

This experience showed me that even complex systems become understandable with enough effort and persistence.

---

## System-Level Thinking

I learned that industrial systems cannot be fixed by focusing only on code.

Problems can come from:
- wiring
- communication protocols
- hardware design
- data processing logic

To solve issues, it is necessary to analyze the entire system, not just a single component.

---

## Value of Each Role

At first, I thought that my role was simple and that anyone could do it.

But after working on real production systems, I realized that each engineering role requires deep understanding and experience.

The same system that looks simple to one engineer may look extremely complex to another.

---

## Working Without Documentation

A major part of this project involved working without proper documentation.

This required:
- reverse engineering
- testing hypotheses
- learning tools independently
- making decisions under uncertainty

---

## Real Engineering

The most valuable part of this project was not writing code, but:

- understanding the system
- finding root causes
- making the system stable
- seeing real results in production

---

## Motivation

I enjoy working with PLC systems, electrical engineering and industrial automation.

This project confirmed that I want to continue developing in this field and work on more advanced systems in the future.
